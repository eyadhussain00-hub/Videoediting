# What if we terraformed Mars? — Treatment

A 60-second vertical reel (1080×1920, 24 fps), English captions, music and sound only (no voice).
Inspired by the format of a "what if humans disappeared" timelapse reel: one world, a time counter,
big time jumps. Our topic, world, shots and wording are our own.

Three versions share this story, timeline and captions (`shared/timeline.json`):

1. `tiltshift/`: Tilt-Shift Miniature (built first)
2. `iso/`: Isometric Infographic
3. `blend/`: Isometric diorama seen through a tilt-shift lens with time-lapse stutter

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. Exponential clock, one locked world** (the reel's grammar): a single crater, the counter jumps
  ×10 each time (1 year → 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 → 100,000), and the world turns red → white → blue →
  green. Clear, made for small screens, and the counter is the hook.
- **B. Before/after pairs**: six split-screen pairs (dust/lake, dome/forest…). Readable, but no build-up
  and no single world to care about.
- **C. One colonist's family tree**: a helmet handed down through generations, until the last one
  takes it off. Emotional, but it needs character acting at tiny scale, which tilt-shift does poorly
  until the very end.

**Chosen: A, with C's ending.** The exponential clock carries the film; the last beat drops to real
time
on one tiny person at the old landing site, who takes the helmet off. That's where the "time-lapse
→ ×1 REAL TIME" trick (Tilt-Shift's native move) earns its place.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A dead red crater becomes a place you can breathe in, in 60 seconds and 100,000 years.

- **Setup** (0–10 s): a lander falls into an empty crater under a butterscotch sky. Day 1.
- **Build** (10–33 s): domes, factories, warming, melting, the first rain.
- **Turn** (33–46 s): life takes over; the domes come down; the sky turns blue.
- **Ending** (46–60 s): time slows to real speed. A tiny figure next to the old lander, now a
  monument, takes off their helmet and breathes. The lens un-tilts; the question to the viewer.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Kim Stanley Robinson's Mars trilogy** (*Red / Green / Blue Mars*). Learn: the colour stages as the
  story's spine. Don't take: names, characters, places.
- **Keith Loutit's tilt-shift films.** Learn: high vantage, real cities turned into toys, sped-up
  rhythm cut on the beat. Don't take: shots or music.
- **The reference reel.** Learn: the counter with "+N YEARS" punch-ins, one-line captions, a closing
  question. Don't take: its city, bridge, captions or type.

Camera moves: slide, push, band rack, crane, track, band narrowing, un-tilt.
Signature shot: shots 5–6, the speed drop onto one figure who takes off the helmet, and the lens un-tilting.

**Checked against the library demo** (DIRECTOR §11): structure (exponential clock over 100,000 years vs one
morning), opening (a lander falling into an empty crater vs a dawn light trail), camera path (no top-down,
no dive, no rise), score (additive layers per era, minor → major vs event-gated skeleton) and ending (stay
at eye level and un-tilt vs rise to a pattern) differ. Only the speed drop is shared; it's the style's core
trick and the story needs it.

Science notes (kept honest for a "what if"): Mars last had liquid water on the surface about 3+ billion
years ago; breathable oxygen from plants alone is usually estimated at ~100,000 years; warming with
greenhouse gases and melting the ice takes about a century in the boldest plans.

## 6. Cue map

- **Tempo** 100 BPM throughout; density, not tempo, accelerates.
- **0–4.8** (bars 0–2): single high glockenspiel pulse + low drone; touchdown hit on bar 1 beat 3.
- **4.8–26.4** (bars 2–11): additive marimba pattern in D minor (red Mars), one voice added at each jump (+1 YEAR: 2nd marimba;
  +10: vibraphone chords; +100: woodblock pulse). Every jump lands on a downbeat with a bell hit.
- **26.4–28.8** (bar 11): near silence; tape-stop of the ensemble, only wind.
- **28.8–45.6** (bars 12–19): rain enters; the theme returns in D major (kalimba + celesta + pizzicato),
  densest at bar 16–19 (eighths); the +10,000 jump is the peak.
- **45.6–50.4** (bars 19–21): tape-stop as the speed ramp falls to ×1.
- **50.4–52.8**: silence; helmet seal hiss at 51.6, one breath, birdsong.
- **52.8–60** (bars 22–25): music-box version of the theme, now in major, as the lens un-tilts; final bell on 55.2 with the end card.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing | thin wind, sand hiss | retro-rocket roar → touchdown thump | glock pulse + drone |
| Base | wind; sped-up granular hiss | relay clicks on each world step, dome "pop" per dome | marimba phasing |
| Warming | wind, distant machine hum | chimney puffs | + vibraphone |
| Melt | wind softer | ice crack, trickles | + woodblock, then tape-stop |
| Rain | rain bed (J-cut: starts under the silence) | thunder roll far away | warmer theme |
| Green | rain → birds | leaf rustle per tree pop | densest |
| Real time | quiet breeze, birds | helmet seal hiss, a breath | silence |
| Un-tilt | breeze, lapping water | | music box (major), final bell |

Sound as transition: rain starts half a bar before the rain caption (J-cut); the birds of the real-time
beat start under the tape-stop (J-cut).

## 8. Titles and captions (Tilt-Shift)

- Captions are **colony signage**: a sign panel (dark slate with a safety-orange edge, like a mission
  placard), Overpass Bold, centred low inside the lower blur band.
- **HUD** at the top: `TIME ON MARS` label and a big monospace counter (Overpass Mono), and the
  speed readout under it (`×86,400 TIME-LAPSE` → `×1 REAL TIME`).
- **Jumps**: "+10 YEARS" punches in big at frame centre for ~1.2 s on the downbeat.
- Title and end card sit high in the sky band where nothing blocks them.
